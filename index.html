How does a computer find the right paragraph?

Retrieval is like a librarian who has never read your question before. It cuts a long document into small pieces, turns each piece into a point in “meaning space”, and hands back the pieces closest to your question. This site lets you see each step happen.

2 · Try

Everything runs on Plaid House, a tiny made-up dorm guide (about 950 words), so you can see every piece at once.

3 · Do it yourself

Then repeat the idea on the real handbook in your notebook. Your results, your explanations.

AEmbeddings and similarity

Similar meaning, similar direction

An embedding model turns text into an arrow (384 numbers for MiniLM). Texts that mean similar things point in similar directions. “How close?” can be measured three ways. Drag the sliders and see when they disagree.

Cosine

Looks only at the angle between arrows. A short passage and a long one compete fairly.

Dot product

Angle and length. A longer arrow can win even if it points a bit away.

Euclidean (L2)

The straight-line gap between the arrow tips. Length matters here too.

Quick check: all your arrows have length 1. You switch from cosine to L2. What happens to the ranking?

Btop-k · how many results should you return?

More results: find more, but more noise

Precision asks “of what came back, how much was useful?” Recall asks “of everything useful, how much came back?” Slide k and watch them pull in opposite directions.

BA different kind of index

Search questions, return their chunk

Instead of searching passages, search a list of questions people already asked. Each stored question carries one attached chunk. Your query finds similar questions; you get back their chunks.

CUsing an LLM to grade retrieval

Can a model grade the results?

Checking every search result by hand is slow. An LLM judge reads a question and one result, then gives a score. You decide what it scores.

1

Input

A question and a passage that search returned.

2

Construct

The one quality being rated, for example “does this passage help answer the question?”

3

Output

A score, such as 1 to 5, that you can count and compare.

Someone's judge rates “is this passage factually true?”. Why is that weak for grading search results?
